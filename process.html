Method

How a perfume gets made here

From a sentence on the wall to a numbered flacon: the whole path, with nothing redacted.

Step 1 of 6

The brief is a question, not a deck

Every Nocturne perfume starts as a sentence the perfumer cannot stop thinking about. "What does wet stone smell like." "What is the sound of a room where nobody is speaking yet." The sentence is written on the wall and stays there until the perfume answers it.

Step 2 of 6

Sourcing, then arguing

Materials are sourced close where it matters: iris from one Tuscan broker, vetiver from one distillery, tuberose from a farm we visit every February. Samples are smelled against reference vials, and the reference vial is never replaced, only resealed.

Step 3 of 6

The trial organ

Trials are composed in grams on a forty-one-material organ. A trial is smelled at hour one, hour four, and hour twenty-four. Anything that cannot hold a conversation across all three is dissolved and the notebook page torn out.

Step 4 of 6

Maceration, four weeks minimum

Concentrate rests with alcohol for at least four weeks in cool darkness, tasted weekly against the formula reference. The calendar is a suggestion; the blotter is the authority.

Step 5 of 6

Filtering and pressing

Batches are chilled-filtered, filled by hand, and numbered. A run is 200 to 600 flacons. Every flacon carries its batch date on the base.

Step 6 of 6

The listening year

For twelve months after release the perfumer wears nothing else on alternating days. Adjustments, when they come, are announced honestly in the journal, version numbered like software.

The rule

The calendar is a suggestion. The blotter is the authority.

It is stenciled above the still room door. Visitors are invited to check whether we obey it. We do.
