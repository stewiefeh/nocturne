Nocturne

The house

One room, one perfumer, six perfumes

Nocturne occupies the annex of a glassworks that stopped making glass in 1961. The still room is the old casting hall. The office is the old loading dock. Everything else is bottles.

The perfumer, Mireille Vance, worked for two large houses before founding this one, and describes both experiences with the warmth of a woman describing a long bus ride. What she kept from those years is method: meticulous formulation, ruthless hygiene, exact batches. What she left behind is the calendar: launches per year, briefs per quarter, flankers per flanker.

Here a fragrance exists because it kept asking to exist. First Rain took eleven months and is the house's most popular perfume. Cedar Silence took six years and is the quietest seller. Both are treated identically: they sit in the cabinet until they are done.

"I have six children and they are all slow. That is the whole business model." — M. Vance, to a trade journal that regretted asking

The years

1987

Mireille Vance buys a deconsecrated glassworks annex with a borrowed still and eleven materials.

1994

Ink Violet is pressed. Four hundred flacons sell out in nine days and the house stops advertising, permanently.

2003

Amber Vigil, composed for a friend through a long winter, joins the cabinet by public demand.

2016

Cedar Silence is released after six years of rejection by its own author. She now concedes it was ready in year five.

2021

Vesper Citrus proves citrus can last. The perfumer writes a journal entry titled "I was wrong about immortelle."

2026

The organ holds forty-one materials. The wait list for a sitting is eleven weeks. The still is thirty-eight years old and runs daily.
